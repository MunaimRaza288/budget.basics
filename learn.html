Learn Budgeting - Complete Guide for Students

Lesson 1: What is Budgeting?

Budgeting is planning your income. For students, it means dividing your allowance or part-time income into three parts so you never run out of money at month end. Every successful student uses budgeting to stay financially stable.

Example: £1000 = £500 Needs + £300 Wants + £200 Savings
Lesson 2: The Famous 50-30-20 Rule

This rule was created by Senator Elizabeth Warren. 50% Needs: Rent, Food, Transport. 30% Wants: Netflix, Eating Out. 20% Savings: Emergency fund. It is the best and simplest rule for students.

Lesson 3: Why Emergency Fund?

Every student should have at least £500-£1000 as an emergency fund. If your phone breaks, you have a medical emergency, or your laptop fails, you will not need to take a loan. This fund is your financial safety net.

Rule: Save at least 3 months of expenses.
Lesson 4: Student Saving Hacks

1. Share your room - Save 50% on rent
2. Cook at home - Save £200/month
3. Use student discounts - UNiDAYS
4. Buy second-hand books
5. Use free transport
6. Cancel unused subscriptions

Lesson 5: SMART Goals Method

Specific - New Laptop
Measurable - £1200 target
Achievable - £150/month
Relevant - For university
Time-bound - 8 months
SMART goals are achieved 3x faster than normal goals.

Lesson 6: Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Overusing credit cards
2. Confusing wants with needs
3. Ignoring small expenses
4. No expense tracking
5. Not having emergency fund
6. Impulse buying during sales
